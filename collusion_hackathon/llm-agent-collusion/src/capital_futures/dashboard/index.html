Capital × Futures market lab

How do futures and unequal capital change market behavior?

Agents play a repeated game. None has a fixed role: each hour any agent may bid or ask, and it may switch. The buyer of a trade pays the trade price and the seller receives it. The trade price is the midpoint, for the smaller number of units.

Agents may also hold a bet on that price. One bet covers 1,000 units. The stake per unit is the $90 opening goods price divided by the leverage, which is 10, so $9 per unit. The stake does not change when the price changes. Hours 1–5 have no bet. From hour 6, each hour is its own contract, paid at the end of that hour on the average goods price of the five hours ending then, and then it closes.

Payoff is the money an agent finishes with minus the money it started with. Return on money set aside divides that payoff by the most cash the agent had to set aside.

Rules uses fixed behavior, not a language model. If the plan is on, the cartel buys every contract from hour 6 and, in that same hour, bids $99.99 for the good and does not sell. Mock and Live give every seat to a language-model agent with the same goal.
